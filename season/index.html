Dodge

    Watch the technique

    1. 1 Dodge

      Drill: Reaction shuffles. A partner throws at 60% from 20 feet, and you dodge without crossing the sideline (stepping out is an out). 3 sets of 10.

      Pitcher cue: Same hips you used to field a comebacker. Side-on, low, light feet.

      Watch the technique

    2. 2 Duck

      Drill: A partner makes a gentle toss at chest height. Move below the path without inviting head contact. If you are unsure about the event's head-contact rules, ask your captain.

      Pitcher cue: It is the pitch out of the zone. Let it go by.

      Watch the technique

    3. 3 Dip

      Drill: Block and hold. Two-hand firm grip, deflect an incoming ball downward, and do NOT let it jar loose (if it hits the ground, you're out; you or a teammate can save it first). 3 sets of 12.

      Pitcher cue: Frame it like you are catching a bunt you will not drop.

      Watch the technique

    4. 4 Dive

      Drill: Learn it with Andrew in person before live speed. Controlled movement only, then return to an athletic stance.

      Pitcher cue: Same footwork you used covering first, under control.

      Watch the technique

    5. 5 Dodge, again

      Drill: Last-man-standing sim. You against 3, one ball each. Bait a throw, dodge, then punish. You WILL be the last one in. Rehearse it.

      Pitcher cue: 2 outs, bases loaded, your game. You have been here.

      Watch the technique

    6. 6 And throw a strike

      Drill: Location ladder. Hit a hips-height target 8 of 10 from midcourt before you add heat. Then coordinated double-throws with a teammate (two balls beat any dodger).

      Pitcher cue: This is the one you already own. Command first, then rip the heater.

      Watch the technique

    05
    The rulebook

    Eight calls. Know them cold.

    It runs on the honor system: call yourself out even when no ref sees it. These follow the WeHo league rules. Tournament rules can differ, so confirm the event's rules with your captain.

    The opening rush

    At the whistle, grab ONLY the balls on your right. No feet-first slides. Rushed balls are dead until you carry them back behind the attack line.

    Catches are gold

    A live catch needs control and both feet in bounds (the full rule covers airborne exceptions). It gets the thrower OUT and brings back the teammate at the front of your line.

    The head rule

    Never aim at a head, and never use yours as a blocker. In WeHo league play, a headshot on a standing player has its own rule; your tournament captain confirms how the event calls it.

    Block, don't drop

    Ball-to-ball blocking is legal. If your block ball gets knocked loose, you or a teammate can still catch it before it hits the ground; if it lands, you're out. A hand or finger hit while blocking is an out.

    Cross the line, you're out

    Touch the floor over the center line, or step out of bounds while dodging, and you're done.

    No stalling

    The published 15-second count is about the team holding the majority of balls, not each player. Use them or lose them.

    No pinch, no 360s

    No pinching the rubber, and no spin-around throws (winding your body a full 360). Normal fastball spin off your fingers is legal. Rip it.

    The honor system

    Call yourself out when hit, even if no ref sees it. Only captains talk to refs. Integrity over a point.
